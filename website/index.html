What Surrounds Us, Shapes Us

A decade of MC3 has shown that childhood conditions are shaped by the relationships, places, systems, and supports surrounding children and families.

For the past 10 years, MC3 has explored the idea that children and youth are shaped by more than individual choices. They are shaped by the relationships they are part of, the neighborhoods they live in, the places they learn and play, and the policies and practices developed by the organizations and institutions that interact with their families.

This dashboard uses verified community indicators to help translate that idea into data. Poverty, graduation, unemployment, child population, and SNAP participation do not tell the whole story by themselves, but together they help describe the conditions surrounding children and families in Monroe County.

MC3 2025 Focus

Looking back at 10 years of childhood conditions, celebrating progress, and asking what partnerships, resources, and strategies are needed for the next 10 years.

Safe, Stable, and Nurturing Relationships and Environments

The CDC's Essentials for Childhood framework emphasizes the importance of safe, stable, and nurturing relationships and environments. When children have access to these conditions, they are more likely to thrive — and thriving children are more likely to grow into thriving adults.

This dashboard connects that framework to local indicators. The goal is not to reduce children's lives to numbers, but to help community partners identify where support, coordination, and resources may be needed.

Relationships

Family, peer, school, and community relationships shape how children experience support and belonging.

Neighborhoods

Housing, transportation, safety, and access to nearby resources influence daily childhood conditions.

Learning and Play

Schools, childcare, after-school programs, parks, and youth spaces shape opportunity and development.

Policies and Practices

Institutions, service systems, and funding decisions shape the supports available to children and families.

Reading the Indicators Together

No single indicator explains childhood conditions by itself. Poverty, employment, education, population, and social service participation should be read together. Poverty and SNAP participation can describe economic pressure. Unemployment provides broader labor-market context. Graduation reflects one part of the learning environment. Child population helps estimate the scale of services needed. Where source files end or older dashboard claims diverge from verified sources, the ETL validation workflow flags those gaps rather than repeating unverified figures.

How Community Partners Could Use This Dashboard

Resource Planning

Child population and SNAP participation could support estimates of the scale of services that may be needed across schools, youth programs, and family supports.

Family Stability

Poverty, unemployment, and SNAP indicators can inform conversations about household economic pressure and the supports families may need.

Education Support

Graduation data may help guide education conversations while noting that newer verified data is needed before using more recent claims.

Cross-Sector Coordination

Shared indicators can support conversations between schools, nonprofits, county services, healthcare providers, and community partners.

Looking Ahead: Continuing the Momentum

The 10th Annual MC3 is both a reflection point and a planning opportunity. With MC3 not budgeted and planned for 2026 because of county budget reductions and YSB's need to focus funds on staff retention and the essential service of running an emergency shelter, the work does not have to disappear. This does not necessarily mean the end of MC3. The momentum can continue through partnerships, resource alignment, and new formats.

This dashboard supports that transition by making verified indicators easier to understand, discuss, and update. It can help community partners continue asking what resources, partnerships, and strategies are needed to make Monroe County the best place to be a child.
